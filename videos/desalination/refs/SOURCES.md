# Sources — «تعمل محطة تحلية إزاي؟»

Style: «لو» (build-along), structure as in `videos/nuclear-shelter/`. Script and drawings are our own.

Facts:
- Boiling: heating water from 25 to 100 °C takes about 315 kJ/kg and evaporating it 2,257 kJ/kg, about 2,570 kJ/kg in
  all, which is roughly 700 kWh for 1,000 litres in a plain pot with no heat reuse. Against about 3 kWh by reverse
  osmosis that is more than 200 times as much.
- Seawater has about 35 g of dissolved salt per litre; a household sediment/carbon filter does not remove dissolved ions.
- RO membrane effective pore size about 0.0001 micron (0.1 nm) against a human hair of roughly 100 micron: about a
  million times smaller (membrane makers' fact sheets; AMPAC, Crystal Quest).
- Osmosis moves water toward the saltier side; seawater's osmotic pressure is about 27 bar, so seawater RO runs at
  roughly 55–70 bar. 60 bar is the water pressure at about 600 m depth (1 bar per 10 m).
- Intakes sit offshore behind screens; pretreatment is media (sand) filters then cartridge filters. Jellyfish and algae
  blooms are known causes of intake and membrane fouling.
- Recovery of seawater RO is about 40–50%, so the brine is close to twice as salty and leaves at nearly full pressure.
- Isobaric pressure exchangers hand that pressure to the incoming feed at 95–98% efficiency
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_exchanger; Energy Recovery Inc. case studies).
- Brine is denser than seawater and sinks; outfalls end in multi-port diffusers that dilute it quickly
  (Jones et al. 2019, "The state of desalination and brine production: A global outlook", Sci. Total Environ. 657,
  https://collections.unu.edu/view/UNU:6695).
- RO permeate has low hardness and alkalinity and is corrosive to pipes; plants remineralise it in limestone (calcite)
  contactors and disinfect with chlorine (Kurita America, "Utilizing Calcite or Limestone Contactors").
- Reverse osmosis produces about 69% of the world's desalinated water (Jones et al. 2019).
- Modern seawater RO uses about 3 kWh per cubic metre (reviews give 2–5); a 1.5 kW air conditioner uses that in 2 hours.
