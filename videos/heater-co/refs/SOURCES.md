# «السخان والحمام المقفول»: sources

## Facts (checked)
- Gas burned with enough oxygen gives carbon dioxide and water vapour; short of oxygen (incomplete combustion) it gives
  carbon monoxide (CO). A yellow or orange flame instead of a blue one is the usual sign of incomplete combustion.
- A small instantaneous (tankless) gas water heater is about 20 kW. It burns about 2 m³ of natural gas an hour, which needs
  about 20 m³ of air, while a small bathroom holds about 8–10 m³. So it burns a bathroom's worth of air in about half an hour.
- Haemoglobin binds CO about 200–250 times more strongly than oxygen. The brain and the heart feel the shortage first.
  Symptoms: headache, dizziness, nausea and vomiting, weakness and confusion, then loss of consciousness.
- CO has no colour, taste or smell. The smell of household gas comes from an odorant (a mercaptan) added to the unburnt
  gas so that leaks can be noticed. It says nothing about CO.
- Carboxyhaemoglobin is bright cherry red, so a poisoned person can look flushed. It is a known sign, but not a reliable one.
- What to do: put the heater outside the bathroom and give it a flue to the outside. If you feel dizzy, open the door, get
  into fresh air and call an ambulance.
