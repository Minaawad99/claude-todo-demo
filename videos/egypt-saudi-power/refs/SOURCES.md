# Sources — «كهربا تحت البحر الأحمر!» (Egypt–Saudi HVDC interconnection)

Facts in the script:
- ±500 kV HVDC, 3,000 MW in two 1,500 MW phases; Badr (Egypt) ↔ Tabuk and East Madinah (Saudi Arabia); ~22–23 km submarine
  cable across the Gulf of Aqaba; ~1,300–1,350 km of overhead lines; about US$1.8 bn; agreement from 2012:
  - https://powerline.net.in/2025/10/24/egypt-and-saudi-arabias-hvdc-interconnection-project-nears-completion/
  - https://www.atkinsrealis.com/en/projects/power-grids-interconnection-project
  - https://www.zawya.com/en/projects/utilities/18bln-power-link-between-egypt-and-saudi-arabia-95-complete-report-ur8bwrlq
  - https://saudienergyconsulting.com/insights/articles/the-saudi-egypt-electricity-interconnection-a-powerful-3-gw-bridge-between-two-grids
- Peak-hour gap of about 3–6 hours; official start targeted mid-November 2026 after the final tests:
  - https://banker.news/154094
  - https://www.aleqt.com/الأخبار/مصر-بدء-تشغيل-الربط-الكهربائي-مع-السعودية-منتصف-نوفمبر-15356
  - https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/2026/08/26/egypt-saudi-arabia-in-final-stages-of-trials-for-3-gw-electricity-interconnection/
- Egypt's grid runs at 50 Hz, Saudi Arabia's at 60 Hz (the reason the link must be DC / back-to-back converted).

Drawings: lattice bipole towers, a converter station (valve hall, converter transformers, switchyard), a cable-laying ship
with a turntable, and a generic HVDC submarine cable cross-section (copper core, insulation, lead sheath, steel armour wires).
