# Sources — «تعمل بريمة حفر إزاي؟»

Style: «لو» (build-along), from a nail to a soil auger. Script and drawings are our own.

Facts:
- A driven nail only displaces and compacts soil; an auger's helical flighting carries the loosened soil up and out
  (auger makers' guides, e.g. Digga "auger wearparts", Earthquake earth-auger bits).
- Parts: pilot point / fishtail (centres the bit so it does not "walk"), replaceable hardened-steel or carbide cutting
  teeth, flighting, and on some models a spring shock absorber against kickback from rocks (Earthquake EA8F product notes; Digga).
- Hand augers with extension rods: 5 m is a standard set, 8–10 m is realistically achievable (Royal Eijkelkamp hand auger
  sets and data sheets).
- Continuous flight auger (CFA) piles: a hollow-stem auger is drilled in, then concrete is pumped through the stem while
  the auger is withdrawn, so the hole is supported the whole time; rigs reach about 30–32 m depth and up to 1.2–1.4 m
  diameter (Soilmec CFA brochure; Balfour Beatty CFA technique sheet; Comacchio rig data).
- The Archimedes screw (in Egypt: the tanbur) has been turned by hand by Nile-delta farmers to lift water since about the
  3rd century BC (Akvopedia "Archimedes screw"; World History Edu).
