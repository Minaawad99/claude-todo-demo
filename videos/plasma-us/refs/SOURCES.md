# «How does plasma donation work?» — sources (American English «اسكتش» episode)

## Facts in the script
- Blood is about 55 % plasma by volume, a light amber/yellow liquid; the rest is mostly red cells (Wikipedia «Blood plasma»).
- Donor screening at US plasma centres: weight, blood pressure, pulse, temperature, and a finger stick for hematocrit and
  total protein (FDA 21 CFR 640.63–640.65; centre donor guides).
- Plasmapheresis: one needle, the machine draws whole blood, a centrifuge bowl (Latham-type, thousands of rpm) separates it,
  plasma goes to a collection bottle, red cells are returned with saline through the same needle in repeated
  draw/return cycles; about an hour (FDA; Haemonetics PCS2 / NexSys operator descriptions; Wikipedia «Plasmapheresis»).
- Collection volume by weight up to about 690–880 mL including anticoagulant («almost a liter») (FDA nomogram, 1992 memo).
- US rules allow plasma donation up to twice in a 7-day period; whole blood every 56 days («every two months»).
- Plasma volume is replaced in about 24–48 hours; red cells take weeks (Red Cross / blood-centre FAQ).
- The US supplies about 70 % of the world's plasma for fractionation (PPTA; widely cited).
- Typical pay is about $30–70 a visit («around fifty bucks»), with higher new-donor promotions.
- Plasma-derived medicines: clotting factors (hemophilia), immunoglobulin (immune deficiencies), albumin (burns, shock).
- About 130 plasma donations to treat one primary immunodeficiency patient for a year (PPTA).
