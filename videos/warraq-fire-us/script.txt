Wednesday, November twenty-fifth… at night.
The Ring Road in Cairo, over the Ashara underpass in Warraq… people heading home, like any other day.
Then suddenly… a truck loaded with fuel, and the driver lost control.
It slammed into the concrete barrier… and flipped over.
Fuel poured across the asphalt… and in under thirty seconds, the whole road was on fire.
The fire jumped from car to car, one after another… more than twenty cars.
People inside their cars… didn't make it out.
In the end… thirty lives were lost, and dozens were injured.
So how did the fire spread that fast?
Because it's not the liquid fuel that burns… it's the vapor coming off it.
And that vapor is heavier than air… it creeps along the ground, and slips under the cars.
And the first spark… from a hot engine, or a car's wiring… lights up everything at once.
So if you're ever in that situation… what do you do?
First: leave the car, and get away, upwind, far away… because the tank can explode.
Second: call nine one one, or your local emergency number… and give the exact location.
If someone's clothes catch fire… they need to stop, drop to the ground, and roll.
Cover them with a cotton jacket, or a blanket… to smother the flames.
For the burn… cool running water, for twenty minutes.
A lot of people think toothpaste or ice helps a burn… nope, they make it worse.
Don't pull off clothes stuck to the skin… cover it with a clean cloth, until help arrives.
If there's smoke around you… get low, the clean air is near the floor.
And your phone, in a moment like this… has one job: to call for help.
Not to stand there filming… and block the way for a fire truck that could save someone.
So, bottom line… get away, call, cover, and cool it with water.
Rest in peace to everyone we lost… strength to their families… and a full recovery to the injured.
Send this to someone you love… one day, it might save a life.
