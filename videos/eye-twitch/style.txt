Speak in casual Egyptian Arabic, the everyday Cairo street dialect, like a calm, confident young Cairene teacher explaining to a friend why the eyelid suddenly twitches by itself. Keep exactly the same Cairo accent, voice and energy from the first word to the last word: never drift into Modern Standard Arabic, Gulf, Levantine or a news-reader tone, not even for a single word. Pronounce ث as ت (تلات، التالت، تلاتة) and ذ as د, pronounce ق as a glottal stop (قاعد = 'aa3ed, يقفل = ye'fel), and ج as a hard g like Cairenes. Warm and clear, with quiet amazement on the surprising facts and numbers. Brisk, natural conversational pace with only very short pauses between sentences; don't stop at commas. Emphasize key words like عضلة الرمش, عصب واحد, لوحدهم, البوتوكس, تلات شهور, خمستاشر ألف. Make the opening line intriguing and the question "إيه الحكاية؟" playful.
