# Ants episode: references

- **Story order and hook:** inspired by a short the channel liked ("How do ants always know where the food is?"). Only
  the idea order was taken; the script is ours in Egyptian Arabic and the drawings and direction are our «اسكتش» style.
- **The ant:** worker of the black garden ant (*Lasius niger*) in side view, after "Scheme ant worker anatomy-en.svg"
  (Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA): elbowed antennae (long scape, funiculus with a club), mandibles, compound eye,
  humped mesosoma, one petiole scale, four-segment gaster, six legs from three coxae, tripod gait. Looked at only.
- **House:** a top-down floor plan on the graph paper (walls hatched, door swing, L-shaped counter, sink, spoon of sugar,
  nest in a crack at the foot of a wall).
- **People:** real body proportions, copied `fig()` from `videos/choking/sketch/shots.js`.

## Facts checked
- Table sugar (sucrose) has practically no smell; scouts find food by searching (random walks) and contact.
- Path integration: foragers keep a running vector of distance and direction home; desert ants count steps
  (Wittlinger, Wehner & Wolf, *Science* 2006, "The ant odometer: stepping on stilts and stumps").
- Recruitment trail: on the way home the forager touches the gaster tip to the ground and lays a trail pheromone from
  glands in the abdomen (in *Lasius niger* from the hindgut); nestmates follow it with their antennae; each successful
  forager reinforces it; the pheromone evaporates, so the trail fades when nobody tops it up.
- Shorter route wins without anyone deciding: the double-bridge experiment with Argentine ants
  (Goss, Aron, Deneubourg & Pasteels 1989; Deneubourg et al. 1990).
- Ant mill / "death spiral": army ants that lose the trail follow each other in a circle until they die of exhaustion
  (Schneirla 1944, *American Museum Novitates*).
- Scale: a 3 mm ant walking 10 m covers ~3,300 body lengths; for a 1.7 m person that is ~5.7 km, said as «ستة كيلو».
