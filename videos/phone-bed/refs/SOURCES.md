## Facts checked
- Intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells (melanopsin) measure light rather than forming images, and are most
  sensitive to blue light, around 480 nm. They signal the suprachiasmatic nucleus, the body's master clock.
- In darkness the SCN lets the pineal gland release melatonin; light at night suppresses it.
- Chang et al., PNAS 2015 (Brigham and Women's Hospital / Harvard): 4 hours of reading on a light-emitting e-reader before
  bed, for 5 nights in a row, compared with a printed book. Melatonin was suppressed and its rise was delayed by more than
  1.5 h. The readers took longer to fall asleep and were less alert the next morning.
- Night-shift and blue-filter modes only partly cut the blue light. A 2021 BYU study (Duraccio et al.) found no
  better sleep with Night Shift alone.
- Melatonin rises at night in day-active and night-active animals alike (for example rats). It signals darkness, not sleep.
