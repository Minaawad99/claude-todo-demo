Facts:
- Ice + salt bottoms out near the salt-water eutectic, about −21 °C (Frostburg State chemistry FAQ; NOAA).
  https://antoine.frostburg.edu/chem/senese/101/measurement/faq/zero-fahrenheit.shtml
- The dasher scrapes ice off the freezing-cylinder wall to keep crystals small; pump/gravity hopper keeps mix refrigerated
  (~4 °C / 40 °F) and feeds the cylinder; an air tube / pump sets overrun (Taylor: "how does a soft serve machine work").
  https://www.taylor-company.com/how-does-a-soft-serve-machine-work/
- Overrun typically 35–45 % → air is roughly 26–31 % of the volume, "close to a third" (Taylor: what is overrun).
  https://www.taylor-company.com/what-is-soft-serve-overrun-and-why-does-it-matter/
- Consistency is sensed through the beater-motor load (amperage); soft serve is served around −6 °C (low 20s °F, patent
  US5706720 and Wikipedia "Soft serve"), against −18 °C in a home freezer.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_serve
- Heat treatment: all mix heated to ≥ 150 °F (66 °C) for ≥ 30 min daily, then cooled; whole cycle about 4 h; full brush
  clean every 14 days on Taylor pump/heat-treat models; lockout if the cycle fails or cleaning is overdue
  (7 Pa. Code § 39.54; Taylor C708/C606 listings; EP4445743).
  https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/pennsylvania/7-Pa-Code-SS-39-54
