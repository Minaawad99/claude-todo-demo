## Facts checked
- Recommended repellent ingredients: DEET, picaridin, IR3535, oil of lemon eucalyptus (OLE) / PMD, 2-undecanone (CDC / EPA,
  https://www.cdc.gov/mosquitoes/modules/mosquito-bites/mosquito-bite-prevention-us-content.html).
- CDC does not recommend pure lemon eucalyptus essential oil, only the registered OLE / PMD product; OLE / PMD not under 3
  years; no repellent on babies under 2 months (net instead) (CDC traveller sheet,
  https://www.cdc.gov/mosquitoes/pdfs/MosquitoBitePreventionTravelers_508.pdf).
- Repellents wear off over hours and with sweat or water; reapply as the label says.
- Soap: Virginia Tech, *iScience*, 10 May 2023 (Lahondère lab). Four volunteers, four soaps; some soaps raised some people's
  attractiveness, Native (coconut scented) tended to repel; results varied by person.
- Fan: Hoffmann & Miller, *J. Med. Entomol.* 2003, "Reassessment of the role and utility of wind…": fan wind cut catches
  at CO₂-baited traps mainly by diluting the attractants. The script says the wind scatters the breath and skin smell.
- Citronella candles: small, short-lived effect (published field trials; general knowledge, not re-searched).
- Vitamin B1 and ultrasonic devices: trials found no effect on bites (Ives & Paskewitz 2005; Cochrane review of
  electronic mosquito repellents, Enayati et al. 2007). General knowledge, not re-searched.
- Limburger cheese: Knols 1996 (*The Lancet*) and De Jong & Knols (*Parasitology Today* 1996): *Anopheles gambiae*, a
  malaria mosquito, is drawn to foot odour and to Limburger cheese, whose smell is close to it (2006 Ig Nobel, biology).
