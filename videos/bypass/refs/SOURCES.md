# «تحويل المسار» — sources

## Facts (checked)
- Roux-en-Y gastric bypass: a pouch of about 30 ml is stapled off the top of the stomach; the jejunum is divided about 50–100 cm past the duodenum; the lower end (Roux limb, 100–150 cm) is joined to the pouch; the duodenal limb carrying bile and pancreatic juice is rejoined lower (the Y). Nothing is removed; the remnant stomach stays and keeps secreting. (ASMBS patient material; StatPearls "Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass".)
- Why it works: restriction, modestly less absorption, and gut hormones (GLP-1, PYY) released when food reaches the distal bowel early; type 2 diabetes often improves within days, before weight loss. (Rubino; STAMPEDE trial.)
- Excess weight loss about 60–70%.
- Costs: dumping syndrome after sugars (dizziness, sweating, palpitations); lifelong vitamins, iron, B12, calcium (more than after a sleeve); marginal ulcer at the join, more with smoking and NSAIDs.
- One-anastomosis («mini») bypass: a long narrow pouch and a single loop join.
- Small intestine is about 6 m long.
- Edward Mason (Iowa) introduced gastric bypass in 1966, after noting weight loss in patients who had part of the stomach removed for ulcers.
