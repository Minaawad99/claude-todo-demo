# «بنزين 80 ولا 92 ولا 95؟» — sources

## Facts (checked)
- Octane number = resistance to auto-ignition (knock), measured against iso-octane (100) and n-heptane (0). Not energy content. — Wikipedia «Octane rating»; SAE / ASTM D2699 (RON).
- A litre of 92 and 95 carries almost the same energy (~32–34 MJ/L); higher octane does not add power to an engine not tuned for it. — Wikipedia «Gasoline» (energy content), US DOE / FTC consumer notes on premium fuel.
- Petrol engines compress the mix to roughly 1/10 (typical 9.5–11:1; high-compression NA and sports engines 12–14:1). Compression heats the gas (adiabatic). — Heywood, *Internal Combustion Engine Fundamentals*.
- Knock: the end gas far from the plug self-ignites before the flame front arrives; the pressure waves make the pinging sound; heavy/long knock can erode or crack pistons. Modern ECUs use a piezo knock sensor bolted to the block and retard the spark, costing power and economy. — Bosch *Automotive Handbook*; Wikipedia «Engine knocking», «Knock sensor».
- The required grade is in the owner's manual and often on a sticker inside the fuel-filler flap.
- Egypt sells 80, 92 and 95 RON; 98 is sold in Europe and the Gulf.
- WWII: 100-octane fuel let the RAF run Merlin engines at higher boost (Battle of Britain, 1940). — Wikipedia «Avgas», «Rolls-Royce Merlin».
