# Onion episode: visual references and facts

- **Hand:** «طب إزاي؟» reference sheets `v04` (head in profile, brain inside) and `v20` (skin cut with nerve endings,
  the receptor «gate» in the surface), `videos/_reference/tabizzay/sheets/`.
- **Tear gland:** Gray's Anatomy fig. 895, «The lacrimal apparatus» (public domain, Wikimedia Commons `Gray895.png`):
  a lobed gland above the outer corner of the eye, its ducts opening into the upper lid. Looked at only.
- **Onion bulb cut lengthwise, onion epidermis cells, the eye in section:** drawn from standard botany/anatomy
  references (fleshy scales meeting at the basal plate with roots under it, papery outer skin; long staggered brick-like
  epidermis cells with a nucleus; cornea, iris, lens, optic nerve). Wikimedia Commons was rate-limiting (HTTP 429) during
  this session, so no other photo was downloaded.
- **Facts checked:**
  - cutting breaks the cells; the sulfoxides (isoalliin) sit in the cytoplasm and alliinase in the vacuole, so they only
    meet when the cell is broken;
  - alliinase makes 1-propenesulfenic acid; lachrymatory-factor synthase (LFS) turns it into the volatile
    syn-propanethial-S-oxide within seconds (Imai et al., *Nature* 2002, House Foods, Japan);
  - the gas activates TRPA1 on the corneal sensory nerves, the same receptor that mustard / wasabi oil (allyl
    isothiocyanate) excites; the reflex drives the lacrimal gland;
  - the «sulfuric acid in your eye» claim is a myth: the gas itself irritates the nerves;
  - cold slows the enzymes and the gas's evaporation; a sharp knife breaks fewer cells;
  - tearless onion by silencing the LFS gene: Crop & Food Research (New Zealand) with House Foods (Japan), Eady et al.,
    *Plant Physiology* 2008.
