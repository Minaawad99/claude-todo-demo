# Sources — مدن ابتلعتها الأرض والبحر
1. Heracleion (Thonis), Abu Qir Bay — Franck Goddio / IEASM, found 2000; colossal statues, dozens of ships (Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thonis-Heracleion).
2. Pompeii — Vesuvius, 79 AD; excavations from 1748.
3. Port Royal, Jamaica — 7 June 1692 earthquake, about two-thirds sank.
4. Alexandria's royal quarter / Antirhodos — submerged by subsidence, earthquakes and the 365 AD tsunami (Goddio, 1990s).
5. Shicheng (Lion City), China — flooded 1959 for the Xin'an River dam, 26–40 m under Qiandao Lake.
6. Plymouth, Montserrat — Soufrière Hills eruptions from 1995; abandoned 1997, buried by ash and mud.
7. Pavlopetri, Greece — submerged Bronze Age town (~5,000 years), streets and tombs.
8. Baiae, Italy — Roman resort sunk by bradyseism (Campi Flegrei); underwater archaeological park.
9. Herculaneum — buried by pyroclastic flows ~20 m deep, 79 AD; carbonised wood and scrolls (Villa of the Papyri).
10. Villa Epecuén, Argentina — flooded November 1985, re-emerged from 2009.
11. Helike, Greece — 373 BC earthquake and sea; remains located from 2001 (Soter & Katsonopoulou).
12. Canopus, Abu Qir Bay — Goddio excavations.
13. Dunwich, England — lost to coastal erosion and storms from 1286 onwards.
14. Atlit Yam — submerged Neolithic village off Atlit, about 9,000 years old.
15. Ravenser Odd, England — destroyed by storms in the 1350s–60s.
