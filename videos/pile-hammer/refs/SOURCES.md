## The same topic in the references: r05 (avoided)
r05 builds a diesel pile hammer: nail → huge steel pile → a falling weight → explosives → petrol → diesel, guide rails,
and its odd fact is compression ignition; seaside setting, red-hatched cut-away cylinder, a yellow crane. Ours goes another
way: a building on soft Nile clay → concrete piles → a wooden tripod with men on the rope → a winch → a steel leader mast →
a helmet and plywood cushion → the self-lifting diesel ram → the soft-soil stall and cold blows; our odd fact is Venice's
hand-driven wooden piles; the blow count tells the engineer the capacity. Compression ignition is told as how the fix works,
not as the surprise. No line taken from r05.

## Facts
- Piles carry buildings on soft ground; concrete piles of ~20 m are common (FHWA driven-pile manual).
- Drop hammers: a weight lifted by rope/winch and dropped; diesel hammers: the falling ram compresses air in the
  cylinder, diesel is injected and ignites from the heat, the blast throws the ram up again; ~40–60 blows/min.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pile_driver · https://vulcanhammer.info/diesel/
- Leaders (a mast) guide the hammer so it strikes the pile squarely.
- Concrete piles need a helmet with a cushion, usually several layers of plywood, to stop the head spalling.
  https://pilebuck.com/pile-driving-by-pile-buck/chapter-3-installation-equipment-2/ ·
  https://library.ctr.utexas.edu/hostedPDFs/tti/33-4.pdf
- Diesel hammers need rebound and are hard to start/run in very soft soil; operators use a few "cold blows" until there is
  resistance, then fire. https://www.eng-tips.com/viewthread.cfm?qid=110287 · https://vulcanhammer.info/diesel/ ·
  TM 5-3895-363-13&P (https://constructionpiledriver.tpub.com/TM-5-3895-363-13P/css/TM-5-3895-363-13P_29.htm)
- The advance of a pile per blow (the set / blow count) is used to judge its bearing capacity.
  https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/infrastructure/geotechnical/05159/chapter3.cfm
- Venice stands on wooden piles driven by hand; Santa Maria della Salute sits on over a million piles (counts quoted:
  ~1,000,000; 1,106,657; 1,156,627 — the script says "أكتر من مليون"). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_della_Salute ·
  https://www.britannica.com/technology/How-Was-Venice-Built-on-Water
