## Facts checked

- A fair-weather cumulus about 1 km on a side (10⁹ m³) at about 0.5 g of liquid water per m³ holds about 500 tonnes
  (NCAR's Peggy LeMone estimate, quoted by USGS and others). Some sources use 0.25 g/m³ and get 250 tonnes.
- An adult African bush elephant is about 5 tonnes, so 500 tonnes is about 100 elephants.
- Cloud droplets are about 20 microns across and sink about 1 cm/s; a hair is about 70 microns.
- Air at cloud height is about 1 kg/m³, so the air in that cubic kilometre is about a million tonnes: the water is
  about 0.05% of it (less than 1/1000).
- Updraughts under a cumulus run about 1 m/s or more, far faster than the droplets sink.
- A raindrop about 2 mm across holds the water of about a million 20-micron droplets (100× the width → 10⁶× the volume).
- Water vapour is invisible; a cloud is liquid droplets and ice crystals.
- A large cumulonimbus can hold of the order of a million tonnes of water.
