Facts (checked 2026-11-19):
- What the crust is (mucus, oil, dead skin cells, dust), why it forms at night (no blinking to wash it away), why it gathers in the corners and dries; more with allergies and dry eye; yellow/green with redness and stuck lids points to infection:
  Cleveland Clinic — https://health.clevelandclinic.org/why-your-eyes-are-crusty-in-the-morning/ ·
  University of Utah Health — https://healthcare.utah.edu/healthfeed/2025/10/morning-eye-gunk-normal ·
  Healthline — https://www.healthline.com/health/what-causes-crusty-eyes-in-the-morning ·
  Wikipedia «Rheum» — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheum
- Blink rate about 15–20 a minute (Cleveland Clinic gives 14–17): https://www.healthline.com/health/how-many-times-do-you-blink-a-day ·
  https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/blinking
- Tear film layers (lipid from the meibomian glands, aqueous, mucin), meibomian glands opening on the lid margin, puncta → canaliculi → lacrimal sac → nasolacrimal duct into the nose: standard ocular anatomy (Gray's Anatomy; AAO).
- Reduced reflex/aqueous tear secretion during sleep (closed-eye tear film): Sack et al., Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci 1992.
- The Sandman (Sandmann) folk figure who sprinkles sand into children's eyes: European folklore, E.T.A. Hoffmann 1816, H.C. Andersen «Ole Lukøje» 1841.
