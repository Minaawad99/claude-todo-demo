# «ليه بنقدّم الساعة في الصيف؟»: references and fact checks

## Drawings (looked at, not reused)
- **Earth:** real coastlines from Natural Earth 110m (public domain) via `videos/lib/geo.min.js`, tilted 23.4° on its orbit
  with the roll of the orthographic projection. North leans toward the Sun in June, away in December.
- **Cairo Tower:** "Cairo Tower Egypt.jpg" and "Cairo, Tower of Cairo, Egypt, Oct 2004.jpg", Wikimedia Commons. The
  lattice shaft, the lotus-shaped crown under the round floors, the deck and the mast. 187 m tall.
- **Power plant:** a gas-fired combined-cycle plant (turbine hall, heat-recovery boiler, single tall stack), as in the
  plants Egypt's grid runs on; "Combined Cycle Power Plant.jpg", Wikimedia Commons.
- **Pylon:** "Herbstein Electricity Pylon Lattice Steel Pylon.png", Wikimedia Commons: tapering lattice body with
  X-bracing and three cross-arms with hanging insulators.
- **People:** real body proportions from `videos/choking/sketch/shots.js` (Drillis & Contini). The children are drawn at
  0.72–0.74 of adult height (about 1.3 m against 1.75 m). The bed is 1.15 H long.
- **Style:** «طب إزاي؟» reference sheets v06 (sun, clock, day line) and v14 (globes).

## Facts checked
- Earth's axial tilt about 23.4° (said «حوالي 23 ونص»).
- Cairo, standard time (UTC+2): 21 June sunrise about 4:54, sunset about 18:59, day about 14 h 05 min; 21 December
  sunrise about 6:43, sunset about 16:59, day about 10 h 16 min. With the clock forward: about 5:54 and 19:59; a winter
  sunrise with the clock left forward would be about 7:43–7:52.
- Egypt brought summer time back on 28 April 2023 after seven years, to save energy; it starts on the last Friday of
  April and ends on the last Thursday of October (Ahram Online, Egyptian Streets, timeanddate.com).
- Egypt imports fuel and LNG for its power stations, paid in hard currency (Ahram Online: more than 100 million barrels of
  fuel a year).
- George Vernon Hudson, a New Zealand entomologist, proposed a two-hour daylight-saving shift to the Wellington
  Philosophical Society in 1895, to have more light after his shift work to collect insects (Wikipedia; WTKR).
