# Sources — «بيت بيستخبى تحت الأرض من الإعصار» (tornado-house)

Topic from a video the user sent (a house lowered into a pit on hydraulic jacks before a tornado, «ابنيها بنفسك» series).
Its script was transcribed only to stay away from it: theirs goes hole → 120 t → 4 jacks → soil caves in (concrete
walls + drain) → power cut (generator) → 30 s → wind in the pit → steel doors with grass → 300 km/h → rises. Ours keeps
the idea but its own steps and order: synchronised lowering, guide rails, a load-holding valve on every jack, lowering
by the house's own weight with no power at all (instead of a generator), a sliding lid tested against flying debris,
the pressure/open-windows myth as the «والغريب», and a battery sump pump. Script and drawings are our own.

## Facts (checked)
- A two-storey wood-frame house weighs very roughly 100–200 t with contents; house movers quote ≈ 100 t for a small one.
  We say «حوالي مية طن» → 25 t per corner jack. https://finehomebuilding.com/forum/how-much-does-a-house-weigh-3 ·
  https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20041219/homehay19/what-does-a-house-weigh-some-mental-heavy-lifting
- Synchronised hydraulic lifting of buildings keeps all jacks within ~1 mm of each other with position sensors
  (Enerpac EVO: 22 cylinders under a 2,800 t building held to < 1 mm).
  https://www.enerpac.com/en-us/infrastructure/repairing-a-buildings-foundation-after-an-earthquake ·
  https://www.holmatro.com/hydraulic-solutions/customer-stories/lifting-lowering-and-levelling-buildings/
- A counterbalance / load-holding valve on the cylinder holds the load if a hose bursts.
  https://apps.boschrexroth.com/products/compact-hydraulics/CH-Catalog/pdf/Counterbalance.pdf ·
  https://www.parker.com/content/dam/Parker-com/Literature/Hydraulic-Cartridge-Systems-Division---Europe/HCSE-Websphere-literature/HCSE_LoadControlValves/Parker_HCSE_LoadControlValve_datasheets_HY15-3502-US-EU_English.pdf
- Lowering needs no pump: the load pushes the oil out through a metering valve (how a hand jack or a hydraulic lift
  comes down). «دقيقتين» is our design figure for the speed, not a measured product.
- Tornado warning lead time in the US averages ≈ 8–10 minutes («عشر دقايق»).
  https://weather.com/storms/tornado/news/2021-04-05-tornado-warning-nws-accuracy
- Myth: opening windows to equalise pressure. NWS / SPC: useless and wastes time; the damage is from the wind and the
  debris. https://weather.gov/media/gld/WXSafety/myths.pdf · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_myths
- EF5 winds are above 200 mph (≈ 322 km/h) → «ممكن تعدّي التلتمية كيلو».
- FEMA P-361 / ICC 500 safe-room test: a 15 lb (6.8 kg) 2×4 board fired at 100 mph (≈ 160 km/h) at walls and doors.
  https://basc.pnnl.gov/information/design-windstorm-and-tornado-protection ·
  https://www.ul.com/thecodeauthority/knowledge/icc-500-certified-protective-storm-shelters-and-safe-rooms
- Sump pumps with battery backup keep pits and basements dry when the power is out.
