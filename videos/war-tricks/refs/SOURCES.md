# Sources — خطط حربية غريبة… نجحت فعلًا

1. Operation Mincemeat (April 1943) — body of Glyndwr Michael as "Major William Martin" off Huelva with fake letters; Germans reinforced Greece/Sardinia before Sicily. (Wikipedia "Operation Mincemeat"; Ben Macintyre, Operation Mincemeat.)
2. Ghost Army — National WWII Museum: https://www.nationalww2museum.org/about-us/notes-museum/ghost-army-world-war-ii-how-one-top-secret-unit-deceived-enemy-inflatable ; Smithsonian: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/how-the-ghost-army-of-wwii-used-art-to-deceive-the-nazis-180980336/ — ~1,100 men, inflatable tanks, sound, 20+ deceptions.
3. Bar Lev water cannons — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Lev_Line ; Ahram Online: https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/303275.aspx ; The National — Baki Zaki Youssef, idea from the High Dam work. (81 breaches: single source, not on screen.)
4. Hannibal's oxen — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ager_Falernus ; Polybius 3.93–94, Livy 22.16–17, Plutarch Fabius 6 — ~2,000 cattle, 217 BC.
5. Mehmed II's ships overland — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horn — chain across the Golden Horn, ships hauled over Galata hill on greased logs (number of ships varies; not on screen).
6. Operation Bertram — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Bertram ; IWM: https://www.iwm.org.uk/history/second-world-war/north-africa-campaign/camouflage-helped-allies-win-the-desert-war — dummy tanks (~400 dummy Grants + vehicles), fake pipeline built slowly.
7. Operation Fortitude / FUSAG — fictitious First US Army Group under Patton; 15th Army held at Pas-de-Calais for weeks after D-Day. (Wikipedia "Operation Fortitude".)
8. Navajo code talkers — National WWII Museum; ~400 Marines; the code was never broken. (Wikipedia "Code talker".)
9. Starfish sites — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfish_site — Dobinson, Fields of Deception: ~968 tons of bombs diverted.
10. Pelusium (525 BC) — Polyaenus, Stratagems 7.9: a late, doubtful story; the card says «تروي الحكاية».
11. Operation Copperhead — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Copperhead — actor M. E. Clifton James as Montgomery, Gibraltar and Algiers, late May 1944.
12. Hannibal's snake pots — Cornelius Nepos, Hannibal 10–11; Dickinson College Commentaries: https://dcc.dickinson.edu/node/229 — Bithynia vs Eumenes II, 184 BC (some date it 183). Card cites Nepos.
13. Operation Greif — National WWII Museum: https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/operation-greif-secret-mission-sow-chaos-us-army — Skorzeny's commandos in US uniforms, checkpoint questions, Eisenhower kept at HQ over an assassination rumour.
14. Khalid's desert march (634) — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_ibn_al-Walid ; al-Tabari via Alim — camels made to drink, later slaughtered for water. A partly legendary account (Lynch 2013), so «تروي المصادر».
15. Quaker guns, Centreville (March 1862) — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker_gun ; Encyclopedia Virginia — painted logs in the works Johnston left.
16. Assunpink campfires (2–3 Jan 1777) — Mount Vernon: https://mountvernon.org/george-washington/the-revolutionary-war/10-facts-about-the-battle-of-princeton ; American Battlefield Trust — rearguard kept fires, wheels muffled, Princeton attacked on 3 Jan.
