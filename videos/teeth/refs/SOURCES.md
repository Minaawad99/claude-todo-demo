# «ليه سنانك بتصفر وإنت بتغسلها؟» — sources

## Idea
- The user's upload: an English explainer «You brush twice a day, so why are your teeth still turning yellow?» (7 brushing
  mistakes). Retold in our own script and drawings: four mistakes, the two layers, the myth and the odd fact. Nothing copied.

## Facts (checked)
- Enamel is semi-translucent and the hardest tissue in the body; dentin under it is naturally yellowish, so thinner enamel
  shows more yellow (standard dental anatomy; ADA / Cleveland Clinic patient pages on tooth discoloration).
- Acidic food and drink soften the enamel surface for a while; wait before brushing and rinse with water first
  (ADA advice: about 60 minutes; the script says «نص ساعة لساعة»).
- Hard scrubbing and hard bristles wear enamel at the neck and cause gum recession; soft brush, 45° toward the gum line,
  short strokes, two minutes twice a day (the modified Bass technique, ADA).
- Spit, don't rinse after brushing, so the fluoride stays on the teeth (NHS «Spit, don't rinse» advice).
- Lemon juice erodes enamel; charcoal powder and baking soda are abrasive (ADA statements on DIY whitening).
- Mature enamel has no living cells (the ameloblasts are lost once the tooth erupts), so it can't regrow.
