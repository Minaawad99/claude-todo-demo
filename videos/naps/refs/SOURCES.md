## Facts checked

- A sleep cycle lasts about 90 minutes (usually 80–110). Deep N3 sleep usually starts about 20–30 minutes after you
  fall asleep, and it shows slow, high delta waves (0.5–4 Hz) on the EEG.
- Waking from N3 brings sleep inertia: grogginess and slower thinking, usually 15–30 minutes, sometimes up to an hour.
  This is why naps of 45–60 minutes leave you groggier than 10–20-minute naps.
- Naps of 10–20 minutes improve alertness and mood with the least grogginess (Brooks & Lack, Sleep 2006: 10 minutes
  gave the best balance).
- A full 90-minute nap includes REM sleep. REM is linked to creativity and to processing emotions.
- Napping late in the day lowers the sleep pressure (adenosine) built up during the day, so falling asleep at night comes
  later and the night is shorter.
- NASA Ames, Rosekind et al., «Crew Factors in Flight Operations IX: Effects of Planned Cockpit Rest on Crew
  Performance and Alertness in Long-Haul Operations» (NASA TM-108839, 1994): a 40-minute rest opportunity, about
  26 minutes of sleep on average; performance up 34% and physiological alertness up 54%.
