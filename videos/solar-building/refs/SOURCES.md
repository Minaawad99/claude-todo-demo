# Sources — «تنوّر عمارة بالطاقة الشمسية إزاي؟»

Style: «لو» (`videos/_reference/law/`). The closest reference, r02 (home solar), was read so as NOT to follow it: our
steps, order and odd fact are different (battery, charge controller, tilt, inverter surge, Egyptian dust, heat loss,
LiFePO4; theirs uses series strings, net metering, shading/optimisers and anti-islanding). Script and drawings are our own.

- One modern panel ≈ 450–550 W; an LED lamp ≈ 10 W, a fridge ≈ 100–200 W running, a 1.5 hp air conditioner > 1 kW.
- Charge controllers stop charging a full battery and disconnect the load before a battery is drained (low-voltage disconnect).
- Fixed-tilt rule of thumb: tilt ≈ latitude, facing south in the northern hemisphere; Cairo ≈ 30° N.
- Egypt's grid: 220 V, 50 Hz (alternating 50 times a second).
- Motor start (inrush) current is several times the running current; inverters are sized for the surge.
- Dust: a Cairo-area trial reported conventional panels lost ~25–33 % of efficiency after six weeks without cleaning.
  https://www.scidev.net/global/news/egypts-self-cleaning-solar-panels-shed-desert-dust/
- Heat: output falls ~0.35–0.4 % per °C above 25 °C; a panel at 65 °C loses ≈ 0.4 × 40 ≈ 16 %; a mounting gap lets air
  cool the panel. https://solarcalculator.com.au/solar-panel-temperature/ ·
  https://www.energysage.com/solar/solar-panel-temperature-overheating/
- Batteries: lead-acid ≈ 300–700 cycles; LiFePO4 ≈ 3,000–6,000. https://www.solar-electric.com/learning-center/batteries-and-charging/lithium-battery-technology.html
- Not used: Egyptian net metering (its status after 2025 is unsettled).
