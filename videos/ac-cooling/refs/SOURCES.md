# References — «التكييف بيبرّد إزاي؟»

No photos were downloaded for this episode. The drawings follow the standard shapes of a wall split AC as shown in
service and textbook diagrams: the indoor unit (long box, louver slit, evaporator coil of copper tube through aluminium
fins), the outdoor unit (fan on the front face, finned condenser on the side, service valves), a hermetic compressor, a
capillary tube as the expansion device, and an R-410A refill cylinder.

Facts used: R-410A boils at about −48.5 °C and R-32 at about −52 °C at atmospheric pressure («حوالي خمسين تحت الصفر»);
compressor discharge gas is typically 70–90 °C; refrigerant runs in a sealed loop and is only lost through a leak;
condensate is room humidity condensing on the cold evaporator; Willis Carrier's first air-conditioning system (1902) was
built to control humidity at the Sackett-Wilhelms printing plant in Brooklyn.
