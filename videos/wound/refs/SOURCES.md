# Wound-healing episode: visual references and facts

- **Skin layers:** "Skin layers.png" (Wikimedia Commons, after Madhero88 / OpenStax-style anatomy diagram), looked at only:
  a thin epidermis with a wavy base over the dermal papillae, a thick dermis (papillary over reticular) with a superficial
  vessel plexus and capillary loops into the papillae, a deep artery and vein pair at the border with the fat, fat lobules
  under it, hair follicles running at a slant and coiled sweat glands deep in the dermis. Not reused.
- **Drawing hand:** the skin block of the «طب إزاي؟» pain video (reference sheet `videos/_reference/tabizzay/sheets/v20.jpg`):
  salmon epidermis, light dermis, a red vessel line, beige fat with round lobules.
- **Cells, from their real shapes:** red cells as biconcave discs (face-on with a pale middle, or edge-on); platelets as small
  discs that put out spikes when they stick; fibrin as long fibres across the plug; neutrophils as round cells with a
  nucleus in three lobes; macrophages as large irregular cells with arms and a kidney-shaped nucleus; fibroblasts as
  spindles with an oval nucleus; bacteria as small rods.
- **Facts checked:** a cut vessel constricts within seconds (vascular spasm); platelets adhere to the exposed wall and to each
  other to form a plug; fibrin strands form a mesh that traps red cells (the clot); the dried top of the clot is the scab;
  neutrophils arrive within hours, macrophages dominate from about 2–3 days; vasodilation gives the redness and warmth;
  fibroblasts lay down collagen in granulation tissue with new capillaries (angiogenesis) from about day 3–4; keratinocytes
  migrate from the wound edges under the scab until they meet; remodelling lasts months up to a year or more; diabetes,
  smoking and infection slow healing; a covered, moist wound re-epithelialises faster than one left to dry (Winter 1962 and
  later trials); scar tissue reaches at most about 80% of the original tensile strength and has no hair follicles or sweat
  glands.
