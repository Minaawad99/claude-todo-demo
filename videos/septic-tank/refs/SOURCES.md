Facts:
- Unlined pits collapse in loose soil and let raw sewage reach groundwater; a septic tank must be watertight (US EPA,
  "How to Care for Your Septic System" / Onsite Wastewater Treatment Systems Manual, EPA/625/R-00/008).
- Size: ~1,000 US gal (≈3.8 m³) minimum for a typical 3-bedroom house (~5 people) (EPA manual; most US state codes).
- Drain slope: ¼ in per foot ≈ 2 cm per metre for small house drains (International/Uniform Plumbing Code).
- Two compartments, the first about two thirds of the volume; solids settle as sludge, fats float as scum (EPA manual).
- Inlet/outlet tees (baffles) draw liquid from the clear middle layer between scum and sludge (EPA).
- Anaerobic digestion gives methane and hydrogen sulfide (rotten-egg smell); vented through a stack above the roof (plumbing codes).
- Household use ~150 L/person/day → 5 people ≈ 750 L/day, so a ~4 m³ tank with no outlet fills in about 5 days.
- Effluent leaves through perforated pipes in gravel trenches (drain field / soakaway); soil and soil microbes treat it (EPA).
- Setback from a water well: WHO guidance for on-site sanitation, ≥30 m horizontal distance from groundwater sources (WHO
  Guidelines on Sanitation and Health, 2018); US codes 50–100 ft.
- Pump-out every 3–5 years for a household tank (EPA SepticSmart); heavy bleach and chemicals kill the digesting bacteria;
  don't flush oils, wipes; grass only over the drain field, no trees (EPA SepticSmart).
