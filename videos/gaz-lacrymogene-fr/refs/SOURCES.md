# Sources — «Gaz lacrymogène : comment ça marche ?»

Facts checked before recording:

- CS = 2-chlorobenzylidène malononitrile, a white solid dispersed as an aerosol/smoke, not a true gas; synthesised in 1928 by Ben Corson and Roger Stoughton (Middlebury College, USA), named after their initials. (Wikipedia «CS gas»; CDC/NIOSH Emergency Response Card «Riot control agents»)
- Acts on the TRPA1 pain receptor of nerve endings, the same receptor activated by wasabi / mustard oil (allyl isothiocyanate). (Bessac et al., FASEB J 2009 «Transient receptor potential ankyrin 1 antagonists block the noxious effects of toxic industrial isocyanates and tear gases»)
- Effects within seconds: tearing, blepharospasm, burning, cough, rhinorrhoea, chest tightness; usually resolve within about 15–30 min after reaching fresh air. (CDC «Facts about riot control agents»)
- Particles/vapour heavier than air: stay low; move to fresh air, higher ground, upwind. (CDC; Amnesty France «Gaz lacrymogènes : quels dangers, que dit la loi»)
- Don't rub eyes; flush with plenty of water or saline; remove contact lenses; wash skin with soap and cool water; bag and wash clothes separately. (CDC «Facts about riot control agents»)
- No evidence lemon, vinegar, toothpaste work better than water; milk is not sterile. (Physicians for Human Rights «Lethal in Disguise» 2016; Amnesty France)
- Seek care if breathing difficulty, chest pain or eye pain persist; higher risk for asthma, children, elderly. France: 15 = SAMU, 112 = EU emergency number.
- Chemical Weapons Convention (1993): Art. I.5 bans riot control agents as a method of warfare; Art. II.9(d) allows them for law enforcement including domestic riot control.
- Context: protests against the cost of living in France, tear gas used in Grenoble, Nantes and Lyon (Euronews, 24/10/2026).
