# Airbag episode: fact sources (checked Nov 2026)

- Full inflation of the bag takes about 20–30 ms; sensing takes about another 20–25 ms, so impact to full bag is about 40–50 ms (ScienceABC; Autobytel; Practical Motoring "How does an airbag work?").
- The crash sensor is an accelerometer; frontal bags fire at a deceleration roughly like hitting a solid wall at 10–15 mph (≈16–24 km/h).
- The igniter fires the propellant, which releases nitrogen; older inflators used sodium azide (toxic; KNO3/SiO2 to neutralise the sodium), modern ones mostly guanidine nitrate and other azide-free mixes (patent literature; CBE20255 notebook).
- The bag leaves the module at roughly 150–250 mph (240–400 km/h); script says "up to 300 km/h".
- Vent holes let the gas out as the occupant loads the bag; the white "smoke" is mostly the powder (cornstarch/talc) that keeps the folded bag from sticking.
- Sit at least 25 cm (10 in) from the steering-wheel cover; children under 13 in the back seat (NHTSA guidance).
- Takata: ammonium-nitrate propellant degrades with heat and humidity and can rupture the inflator; ≈100 million inflators recalled worldwide, the largest automotive recall in history (AP).
- Nitrogen is about 78 % of air.
