# Sources — «تعمل طيارة إزاي؟»

The build is a narrow-body twin-jet airliner (Airbus A320 family numbers). Script and drawings are our own.

- Wingspan ~36 m: A320ceo 35.8 m (Airbus A320 specifications).
- Weight ~70 t: A320 max take-off weight 73.5–78 t; a typical take-off weight is around 70 t.
- Take-off rotation speed ~270 km/h: A320 Vr is typically 140–150 kt (260–280 km/h) at normal weights.
- Engine thrust ~12 t: CFM56-5B4 rated 27,000 lbf (≈12.2 tonnes-force); IAE V2527 is similar.
- Fuel ~24,000 L: A320ceo standard fuel capacity 24,210 L, carried in the wing and centre tanks.
- Wing bending relief: fuel in the wings adds weight along the span that counters the upward lift bending moment
  (the reason wing tanks empty last / the "zero-fuel weight" limit exists).
- Tail: horizontal stabiliser gives pitch stability, vertical fin yaw stability (weathercock stability), like arrow fletching.
- Ailerons: deflect in opposite directions to roll the aircraft; the banked lift turns it.
- Cruise ~11 km: FL350–FL390 typical. Time of useful consciousness at FL350 is 30–60 s (FAA AC 61-107B).
- Pressurisation: bleed air from the engines feeds the packs; cabin altitude is kept at or below 8,000 ft (≈2.4 km)
  (14 CFR 25.841).
- Flaps: trailing-edge Fowler flaps slide back and down, adding area and camber so the wing lifts at lower speed.
- Landing: A320 approach speed ~130–140 kt (≈250 km/h); wheel brakes plus thrust reversers.
- Air Transat Flight 236 (A330, 24 Aug 2001): ran out of fuel over the Atlantic, glided ~19 minutes and about
  120 km (65 nmi) to Lajes, Azores; all 306 aboard survived. Airliner glide ratio is roughly 17:1.
- Round windows: square corners concentrate stress (de Havilland Comet lessons).
