# Sources — «تعمل مطار إزاي؟» («لو» style)

## The user's reference video (same series «ابنيها بنفسك!», an airport)
Transcribed and looked at so as NOT to follow it. Theirs: flat ground sprayed with water → 300 t plane → compacted
earth, gravel, concrete → 3 km runway along the wind → slope and grooves for rain → edge lights → ILS (localizer +
glide slope) in fog → tower and radar → taxiways, terminal, jet bridges → «الغريب»: the runway number is the compass
heading ÷ 10 → birds → fuel, fire trucks, terminal.
Ours, different on purpose: we open on stones sucked into the engine (FOD) and the Concorde, a sweeper patrol, the
take-off speed as the reason for the length, the windsock, PAPI lights (not ILS), the 3-minute fire-station rule, and
the «الغريب» is the crushable runway end (EMAS). No grooves, no ILS, no radar, no runway numbers, no birds.

## From the 14 «لو» references (videos/_reference/law/)
- r10 (desert): warm sandy ground and hazy sky instead of grass.
- r13 (crane): parts arriving from above / growing in height (the tower rises floor by floor).

- r14 (drainage): the earth cut open under the build to show the layers.
- r08 (power plant): dark label pills on parts, a red ✗ on what fails.
- r02 (solar): the night version of the scene for the «بالليل» problem.

- Big jets rotate at about 145–180 kt ≈ 270–330 km/h («حوالي تلتمية كيلو»): https://skybrary.aero/aircraft/a388
- Long-haul runways are typically 3–4 km.
- PAPI: four lights, two white + two red = on the 3° glide path: https://skybrary.aero/articles/visual-glideslope-indicators
- ICAO Annex 14 §9.2: rescue & fire-fighting response not exceeding 3 minutes (preferably 2) to any part of the
  movement area: https://skybrary.aero/articles/rescue-and-fire-fighting-services
- EMAS (crushable concrete at the runway end): FAA, 2025 — 24 aircraft stopped in the U.S. («أكتر من عشرين»):
  https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/two-emas-systems-successfully-stop-aircraft-separate-incidents ·
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineered_materials_arrestor_system
