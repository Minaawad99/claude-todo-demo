# Sources — «تعمل بنزينة إزاي؟»

Style: «لو» (build-along), structure as `videos/nuclear-shelter/`. Script and drawings are our own.

Facts:
- A drum is ~200 L and a car tank ~50 L, so about four cars per drum.
- Underground tanks: 10,000 US gal (~38,000 L) is a standard station tank size (makers list 10–20 thousand gallons).
- New tanks must be double-walled with an interstitial leak sensor (US EPA, "Release Detection for USTs — Interstitial Method",
  epa.gov/ust; required for tanks installed after April 2016).
- "One litre of gasoline can contaminate 1 000 000 litres of groundwater": Government of Canada, "Groundwater contamination"
  (canada.ca, Environment and Climate Change Canada). An order-of-magnitude figure.
- Tank vent pipes end at least 12 ft (3.66 m) above ground (NFPA 30 / International Fire Code 3404.2.7.3.3; state fire-marshal checklists).
- Submersible turbine pump inside the tank pushes fuel to the dispensers; light-vehicle dispensers deliver up to ~50 L/min,
  the US caps it at 10 gal (38 L)/min (Wikipedia "Fuel dispenser"); 40 L/min is the usual standard-flow figure.
- Automatic nozzle shut-off: a sensing hole at the spout tip feeds a Venturi; fuel covering the hole trips a diaphragm and the
  valve closes, purely mechanical (Wikipedia "Fuel dispenser"; Hackaday "Tech Hidden In Plain Sight: Gas Pumps"; ScienceABC).
- Breakaway coupling on every hose and a shear (impact) valve under every dispenser (NFPA 30A; state checklists, e.g. Kansas, Pennsylvania 37 Pa. Code 14.3).
- Sealed concrete forecourt draining to a forecourt oil/water separator (Wikipedia "Petrol interceptor"; UK Environment Agency guidance).
- Mobile phones: the Petroleum Equipment Institute has not documented a single fire caused by a phone, and has counted more
  than 150 refuelling fires attributed to static electricity (IEEE Spectrum "Cellphones pose no gas station hazard"; Snopes; TechXplore 2022).
- Emergency stop cuts power to all dispensers (NFPA 30A 6.7).
