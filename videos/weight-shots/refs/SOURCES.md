# «حقن التخسيس بتخسّس إزاي؟» — sources

## Facts in the script (checked)
- GLP-1 is made by enteroendocrine L-cells of the small intestine (mostly the ileum) and colon after a meal (Holst, Physiol Rev 2007).
- Native GLP-1 lasts about 1.5–2 minutes in the blood; the enzyme DPP-4 cuts its first two amino acids (Deacon et al. 1995).
- Semaglutide: alanine 8 → Aib (resists DPP-4) and a C-18 fatty-diacid side chain on lysine 26 that binds albumin; half-life
  about one week, hence one injection a week (Lau et al., J Med Chem 2015).
- Where it acts: the hypothalamus (arcuate nucleus: POMC/CART "fullness" neurons activated, AgRP/NPY "hunger" neurons
  inhibited) and the brainstem; slower gastric emptying; glucose-dependent insulin release from the pancreas (Drucker, Cell Metab 2018).
- Weight loss comes from eating less (energy intake down about 16–39 % in trials, e.g. Friedrichsen et al. 2021); the drug
  does not burn fat directly.
- Lean mass: in the STEP 1 DXA substudy about 39 % of the weight lost was lean mass; reviews give roughly 25–40 %
  (the script says «من ربع لتلت», the low-to-middle part of that range). Protein and resistance exercise are advised.
- Stopping: STEP 1 extension (Wilding et al., Diabetes Obes Metab 2022): one year after stopping semaglutide, people regained
  two-thirds of the weight they had lost (−17.3 % at week 68, −5.6 % at week 120). The graph follows that shape.
- The first drug of the class, exenatide (approved 2005), is synthetic exendin-4, found by John Eng in 1992 in the saliva of
  the Gila monster (Heloderma suspectum), a venomous lizard of the south-western US and north-western Mexico.
