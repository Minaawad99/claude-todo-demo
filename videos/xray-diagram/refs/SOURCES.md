# Sources — «إزاي الأشعة بتشوف عضمك؟» (diagram style)

Style reference: a video the user sent (an Egyptian X-ray explainer in a cream technical-diagram look, series «طب إزاي؟»);
only the look and pacing were copied, the script and drawings are our own.

Facts (standard radiography): a heated filament gives off electrons (thermionic emission); diagnostic tubes run at about
50–150 kV, so the electrons reach over half the speed of light; braking in the tungsten anode (bremsstrahlung) makes the
X-rays, with about 99% of the energy turned to heat, which is why the anode disc spins thousands of rpm; soft tissue (mostly
water) lets X-rays through, calcium in bone absorbs them, so bone stays white on the detector; Wilhelm Röntgen found them in
1895, called them "X" for unknown, and the famous early image is his wife Anna Bertha's hand with her ring.
Drawings: rotating-anode tube cut-away (cathode cup + filament, anode disc, rotor, bearings, window), hand bones (radius,
ulna, 8 carpals, metacarpals, phalanges) from anatomy diagrams.
