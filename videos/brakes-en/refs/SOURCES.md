# «الفرامل» / How Brakes Stop a Car: sources (full review)

## Facts (checked)
- A mid-size car is about 1.5 t. From 100 km/h (60 mph) a modern car stops in about 36–40 m (120–130 ft) of braking.
- The force is multiplied three times:
  - the pedal is a lever, about 4:1;
  - the vacuum booster multiplies the push again, several times;
  - the caliper pistons have more area than the master-cylinder piston, so the same pressure gives more force (Pascal).
  A push of about 30 kg (70 lb) ends as a clamping force of more than a ton.
- Brake fluid is practically incompressible, so the pressure reaches all four wheels together.
- The pads clamp a disc that turns with the wheel. Friction turns kinetic energy into heat. In hard braking a disc can
  pass 500 °C (900 °F). Vented discs have vanes between two faces, so air flows through and cools them.
- The tyres' grip on the road is what finally stops the car. A locked wheel slides and can't steer.
- ABS releases and re-applies the brake pressure up to about 15 times a second, so the wheel keeps turning.
- Electric cars use regenerative braking: the motor works as a generator and sends energy back to the battery.
