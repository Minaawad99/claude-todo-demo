# «مثلث برمودا» — sources

## Facts (Flight 19 and the triangle)
- US Navy Board of Investigation into the loss of Flight 19 and PBM-5 BuNo 59225 (1946), radio log times: take-off 14:10,
  Lt. Cox hears Taylor ~15:40 ("both my compasses are out… I'm sure I'm in the Keys"), "fly west" heard from a student,
  ~18:20 "close up tight… when the first plane drops below 10 gallons we all go down together", last transmission ~19:04,
  PBM Training 49 airborne 19:27, SS Gaines Mills reports flames 19:50; finding amended in 1947 to "causes or reasons unknown".
- Allen Kosnar, the student excused from the flight; later interviews.
- USS Cyclops, lost March 1918 with 306 aboard (Naval History and Heritage Command).
- Vincent Gaddis, "The Deadly Bermuda Triangle", Argosy, February 1964.
- Bruce Gernon's account of 4 December 1970 (Andros → Palm Beach, Bonanza A36, the "tunnel" cloud, 47 minutes), from his
  own book and interviews; told as his account, not as a proven fact.
- Puerto Rico Trench depth ~8,400 m (NOAA); US Coast Guard statement that the area has no more losses than other busy waters.
- 1991 Scientific Search Project: five Avengers found off Fort Lauderdale, not Flight 19.

## Shapes
Wikimedia Commons image downloads were blocked from this container (HTTP 403 on upload/thumb hosts), so the aircraft are
drawn from their documented features: Grumman TBM Avenger (big radial cowling, long greenhouse canopy, ball turret, deep
belly with bomb bay, tall rounded fin; Taylor's plane FT-28), Martin PBM Mariner (two-step boat hull, gull wing, two
engines, twin fins), USS Cyclops (row of coaling gantries), Beechcraft Bonanza (V-tail).

## Map

`film/map.js`. Egypt's outline is shifted by 100° of longitude at the same latitude to compare sizes.
