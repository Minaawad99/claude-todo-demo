# «تكميم المعدة» — sources

## Facts (checked)
- Sleeve gastrectomy removes about 75–80% of the stomach along the greater curve, leaving a tube of about 100–150 ml; laparoscopic through 4–6 small ports; staple line from the angle of His, about 4–6 cm from the pylorus, sized on a bougie. (ASMBS patient material; StatPearls "Sleeve Gastrectomy".)
- The fundus makes most of the circulating ghrelin; levels fall after the sleeve. (Karamanakos et al., Ann Surg 2008.)
- Excess weight loss about 55–70% at 1–2 years; improvement or remission of type 2 diabetes, hypertension and sleep apnoea in many. (ASMBS; STAMPEDE trial.)
- Classic eligibility BMI ≥ 40, or ≥ 35 with an obesity-related disease (NIH 1991); 2022 ASMBS/IFSO guidelines lowered it to ≥ 35 regardless, ≥ 30 with metabolic disease.
- Staple-line leak about 1–2%, bleeding about 1–2%; new or worse GERD common; lifelong vitamins (B12, iron, D, calcium); irreversible; weight regain with high-calorie liquids and grazing.
- Hair loss (telogen effluvium) usually 3–6 months after surgery, from fast weight loss and low protein; temporary, grows back within about a year.
- The sleeve began as the first stage of the duodenal switch for very heavy patients (Gagner and others, around 2000) and became a standalone operation once patients lost enough weight with it alone.
