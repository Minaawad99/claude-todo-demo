## Facts checked

- Hypnic jerks (sleep starts) are sudden, brief contractions of the muscles as you fall asleep, often with a feeling of
  falling; they are harmless (Sleep Foundation, «Hypnic Jerks»; Wikipedia, «Hypnic jerk»).
- Up to about 70% of people get them (Sleep Foundation; TIME, «Why Your Body Jerks When You Fall Asleep», 60–70%).
- The leading explanation is a misfire in the brainstem's reticular (arousal) system during the hand-over from waking to
  sleep (Sleep Foundation; Live Science). The «only one switch on» rule is the sleep-wake flip-flop switch model
  (Saper, Chou & Scammell, Trends in Neurosciences 2001).
- Caffeine and other stimulants, stress and anxiety, and too little sleep make them more likely (Sleep Foundation; TIME).
- It is not known whether the falling feeling causes the jolt or comes with it (the source short; Wikipedia: no theory
  fully accepted).
- The tree theory: psychologist Frederick Coolidge (University of Colorado) proposed an archaic reflex in which the brain
  reads the relaxation of sleep onset as a primate falling out of a tree (Live Science; Radiolab «Still Hanging», 2010).
  It is a hypothesis, not proven, as the script says.
