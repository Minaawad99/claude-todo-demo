# «المضاد الحيوي من غير دكتور»: references

## Drawings («اسكتش» version)
Wikimedia Commons answered 429 (rate limit) in this session, so no photos were fetched. Shapes follow well-known
textbook and micrograph forms, in the hand of the «طب إزاي؟» reference sheets v17 and v20:
- **Bacterium:** a rod with rounded ends and flagella, like *E. coli* (about 2 µm long, 0.5–1 µm wide).
- **Cold virus:** rhinovirus, a 20-sided protein shell about 30 nm across with no envelope. It is drawn small next to the bacterium, though not fully to scale.
- **Cell wall:** peptidoglycan, sugar chains joined by short peptide cross-links. Penicillin and other β-lactams stop
  the cross-links being made, so the wall weakens and the cell bursts from its own pressure.
- **Human cell:** membrane only, no wall, so wall-targeting antibiotics leave it alone.
- **Fleming's plate (1928):** a *Penicillium* mould colony on a staphylococcus culture, with a clear ring around the mould.

## Facts checked
- Antibiotics act on bacteria, not viruses. The common cold is viral (mostly rhinovirus) and clears in about 7–10 days.
- *E. coli* can divide about every 20 minutes in ideal conditions. 8 h = 24 doublings ≈ 16.7 million from one cell.
- Resistance comes from rare mutations plus selection, and from genes passed between bacteria on plasmids (conjugation).
- Antibiotics also kill normal gut bacteria (the cause of antibiotic-associated diarrhoea).
- Fleming, Nobel lecture, 11 December 1945: warned that underdosing could make microbes resistant to penicillin.
- Course length: current guidance (WHO, BMJ 2017 debate) is to take the dose and duration the prescriber sets. The
  script says that, rather than «always finish the box».
