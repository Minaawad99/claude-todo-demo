# Sources — «تبني كوبري فوق الشارع إزاي؟» («لو»)

Style: «لو» (`videos/_reference/law/`), built like «تنوّر عمارة بالشمس» (`videos/solar-building/`).
The topic came from a Gulf «ابنيها بنفسك!»-style video the user sent (wooden posts and plank → piles → precast segments
by truck → launching gantry → post-tensioning → match-cast keys → expansion joint). We avoided its steps on purpose:
ours starts from a hill of earth, then ramps + columns, Nile clay → piles, scaffolding vs precast girders set by a crane
at night, pre-tensioned strands, the camber surprise, the topping slab, rubber bearings, clearance, deck slope + drains,
and the concrete barrier. No launching gantry, no segments, no expansion-joint teeth. Script and drawings are our own.
Our earlier bridge episodes (`bridge-water`, `sheikh-jaber-bridge`) are about piers in water; this one is on land.

What the drawing takes from the references:
- r07 (drill): the auger digging down through the cut-open soil with a darker layer below → our piles reaching firm sand.
- r11 (concrete): the road with a cut-open layered ground under it → our street band over clay and sand.
- r13 (tower crane): the part lowered on a hook → our girders lowered by the mobile crane at night.
- r12 (electricity pole): the pole sinking in the hole close-up → our columns sinking and leaning in clay.
- r08 / r10: the round close-up of a detail → our «مقطع في الكوبري» magnifier (slope, drains, barrier).
- r09 / r14: a red ✗ and a red pill on every failure; a yellow ring on the new part.

Facts (checked):
- Bored piles in Cairo and the Nile valley commonly reach ~18–47 m, ending in dense sand/gravel under silt and clay
  (Rod El Farag axis: up to 31 m; Cairo University report: 30–40 m usual). We say «تلاتين متر».
  https://www.soilmec.com/en/?p=1899 · https://geotechnical-solutions.bauer.de/en/foundation-work-for-the-banha-bridge-over-the-nile-river
- Concrete's tensile strength ≈ 10 % of its compressive strength (range 7–15 %). We say «حوالي عُشره».
  https://theconstructor.org/practical-guide/concrete-under-tension/6805/
- Pretensioned girders: strands are stretched before casting; when released they squeeze the concrete and the girder
  cambers upward. https://wsdot.wa.gov/eesc/bridge/software/Documentation/PGSuper/3.0/tg_camber.html ·
  https://pci.org/PCI_Docs/Design_Resources/Guides_and_manuals/references/bridge_design_manual/JL-11-Winter_Precast_Prestressed_Girder_Camber_Variability.pdf
  The amount is typically a few cm and varies; we say «كام سنتيمتر» and give no exact number.
- A composite topping slab is cast over precast girders to tie them together and set the road profile (same sources).
- Elastomeric bearings: rubber layers with steel plates, letting the girder move with heat and rotate. (Standard practice.)
- Vertical clearance 5.5 m: geometric design table of the JICA study for Cairo urban toll expressways, which follows
  Egyptian standards. https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/11928462_02.pdf
- Deck cross slope 2 % minimum; scuppers carry water to pipes down the piers.
  https://roads.maryland.gov/OBD/CH12-Bridge-Deck-Drainage-Systems-April-2011.pdf · https://iowadot.gov/media/4651/download
- New Jersey barrier: the sloped lower face lifts the tyre and steers the car back onto the road.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_barrier
- Not used: the 5.5 m figure as an exact Egyptian code clause (the code text itself was not found; we say «حوالي»).
