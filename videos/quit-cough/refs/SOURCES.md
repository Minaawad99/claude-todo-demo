# Quitter's cough episode: visual references and facts

Topic idea from a short English explainer the channel sent (on why quitters cough more). Only the topic was taken; the
script, the drawings and the order are our own.

## Real shapes the drawings follow (looked at only, not reused)

- **Airway tree:** Gray's Anatomy fig. 961 «The cartilages of the larynx, trachea and bronchi» (public domain, Wikimedia
  Commons `Gray961.png`). Larynx on top, a trachea of C-shaped rings, the right main bronchus shorter, wider and steeper
  than the left, then the branching bronchi.
- **Lungs:** «Heart and lungs», Gray's Anatomy (public domain, Commons `Heart-and-lungs.jpg`). Right lung in three lobes,
  left in two with the cardiac notch, apexes above the bronchi, concave bases.
- **Airway wall:** OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology fig. 22.8b «The trachea» micrograph (CC BY 4.0, Commons `2308_The_Trachea-b.jpg`)
  and fig. 4.4 pseudostratified epithelium (`2304_Pseudostratified_Epithelium.jpg`): air, cilia on tall columnar cells with
  nuclei at different heights, goblet cells, submucosal glands, hyaline cartilage.
- **Cilia:** «Bronchiolar epithelium 3/4 – SEM», Louisa Howard, Dartmouth (public domain, Commons): dense rods on each cell.
- Reference sheet for the drawing hand: `videos/_reference/tabizzay/sheets/v17.jpg` (lungs and airway tree).

## Facts checked

- CO leaves the blood within about 48 hours of the last cigarette (NHS «Quit smoking» timeline).
- Ussher, West, Steptoe, McEwen, *Tobacco Control* 2003;12:86–88: 174 quitters, cough reports rose significantly after one
  week of abstinence; symptoms faded by about six weeks.
- Dicpinigaitis et al., *Eur Respir J* 2006;28:786–790: 29 smokers, capsaicin (chili) cough challenge; cough reflex
  sensitivity rose two weeks after quitting.
- Smoking raises mucus output (goblet cell hyperplasia) and shortens cilia (Leopold et al., *PLoS ONE* 2009, 9–15% shorter);
  mucociliary clearance is slower in smokers.
- About 200 cilia per ciliated airway cell, beating about 10–20 times a second (standard physiology).
- A cough lasting more than three weeks, or coughing blood: see a doctor (NHS).
