# Sources — «تعمل سكة حديد القطر السريع إزاي؟»

Style: «لو» (`videos/_reference/law/`). The user sent a Gulf-Arabic «ابنيها بنفسك» video that builds the Haramain train
(flange → ballast and sleepers → catenary/pantograph → pointed nose → welded rail → sand dunes → regenerative brakes).
Ours avoids its order and its odd fact: we start from rubber wheels sinking in sand, add the gauge-holding sleepers,
ballast, wheel coning (not the flange), the «تك تك» joints as the surprise, welding, the turnout, in-cab signalling,
electrification, and fencing/grade separation instead of level crossings. No pointed-nose step, no dune step, no
regenerative-brake step.

## Facts (checked)
- Egypt high-speed line 1 (Green line) ≈ 660 km, Ain Sokhna – New Administrative Capital – … – Alamein – Marsa Matrouh;
  design speed 250 km/h, Velaro Egypt in service 230 km/h; 41 eight-car Velaro sets; ETCS Level 2; standard gauge
  1,435 mm; fences in the civil-works scope. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Egypt ·
  https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-mobility-finalizes-contract-2000-km-high-speed-rail-system-egypt
- Traction substations feed the overhead line at 27.5 kV (Egyptian Ministry of Electricity source).
  https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2025/10/7/2868248/
- Status Nov 2026: still under construction/trials; commercial opening targeted 2027 — the script does not say it is running.
- Steel wheel on steel rail rolling resistance ≈ 0.001–0.002 vs a road tyre ≈ 0.01–0.015 → roughly a tenth (textbook).
- Wheel coning (tapered treads) steers a wheelset round curves; the flange is a backstop (textbook, Feynman's railway wheel).
- Steel expands ≈ 12×10⁻⁶ /°C → 1 km heated by 40 °C ≈ 0.48 m («نص متر»); jointed track leaves expansion gaps, the
  source of the clickety-clack; continuous welded rail is stressed at a neutral temperature.
- Stopping from 230 km/h at ~0.7–1 m/s² ≈ 2–3 km («كيلومترات»).
