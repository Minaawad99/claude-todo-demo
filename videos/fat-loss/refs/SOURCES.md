# Fat-loss episode: visual references and facts

- **Hand:** «طب إزاي؟» sheets `v20` (the pink body from the front with inner organs, close cuts with leader labels) and
  `v04`, `videos/_reference/tabizzay/sheets/`.
- **Shapes (standard histology/anatomy):** white adipocytes packed together, each almost filled by one lipid droplet that
  pushes a flattened nucleus to the edge; the triglyceride as a glycerol head with three fatty-acid chains; the
  mitochondrion with its outer membrane and folded inner membrane (cristae); lungs and windpipe in the chest.
- **Facts checked:**
  - Meerman & Brown, «When somebody loses weight, where does the fat go?», *BMJ* 2014;349:g7257: to oxidise 10 kg of
    human fat you inhale 29 kg of oxygen and produce 28 kg of CO2 and 11 kg of water; 8.4 kg of the 10 kg leaves as
    CO2 through the lungs (84%), 1.6 kg as water (urine, faeces, sweat, breath, tears). The narration rounds this to
    «حوالي تمنية ونص» and «الكيلو ونص», and the drawing shows 8.5 / 1.5 to match it; the summary card gives 84%.
  - lipolysis: low insulin and raised adrenaline/glucagon switch on the lipases (ATGL, HSL) that cut the three fatty
    acids off glycerol; the fatty acids travel in the blood (on albumin) to muscle and liver, are broken into two-carbon
    pieces (beta-oxidation) in the mitochondria and burned with oxygen to CO2, water and energy (ATP);
  - fat does not turn into muscle; sweat is water for cooling;
  - adult fat-cell number stays about constant; cells shrink with weight loss (Spalding et al., *Nature* 2008).
