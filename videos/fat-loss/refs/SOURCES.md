# «الدهون بتروح فين لما بنخس؟»: visual references and facts

Real photos and anatomical plates looked at before drawing (Wikimedia Commons, via upload.wikimedia.org; looked at only,
nothing reused). The drawing hand follows the «طب إزاي؟» sheets `v17` (lungs) and `v20` (body, skin cut).

- **Fat cells:** «Yellow adipose tissue in paraffin section – lipids washed out» (packed polygonal cells with thin walls,
  nucleus flattened on the edge) and «Blausen 0012 AdiposeTissue» (yellow droplets, dark nucleus on the rim, fat lobules
  under the dermis). → `fatCell`, `fatField`, `skinCut`.
- **Skin cut:** «Skin.png» (epidermis, dermis, subcutis with fat lobules, hair shafts, vessels).
- **Lungs and trachea:** «Illu bronchi lungs», «Heart-and-lungs» (Gray): three lobes on the body's right, two and the
  cardiac notch on its left, ringed trachea, branching bronchi. → `lungs`, `trachea`, `heart`.
- **Torso and organ places:** «Surface projections of the organs of the trunk», «Male Thorax» photo. → `body` (kidneys,
  ureters, bladder with `organs: true`).
- **Torso from the side:** «Abdominal obesity in men» photo. → `sideBody` on the hook card (the belly outline shrinks).
- **Muscle:** «Blausen 0801 SkeletalMuscle» (bundles of striped fibres, mitochondria between them). → `muscle`.
- **Mitochondrion:** «Animal mitochondrion diagram» (bean, folded inner membrane). → `mito`.
- **Fat molecule:** «Fat triglyceride shorthand formula» (glycerol backbone, three zig-zag tails). → `trig`, `fa`.

## Facts checked

- Meerman & Brown, «When somebody loses weight, where does the fat go?», BMJ 2014;349:g7257: 10 kg of fat leaves as
  8.4 kg of CO2 (84%) and 1.6 kg of water (16%); a 70 kg person at rest breathes out about 200 g of carbon a day, about a
  third of it during 8 hours of sleep; jogging raises the metabolic rate about sevenfold; breathing faster at rest only
  causes hyperventilation (dizziness). The same paper reports that most doctors and dietitians asked thought fat turns
  into energy or heat.
- Fat is stored as triglycerides (carbon, hydrogen, oxygen only).
- Adults keep a roughly constant number of fat cells; weight loss shrinks them (Spalding et al., Nature 2008).
