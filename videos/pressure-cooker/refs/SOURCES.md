# «ليه حلة الضغط أسرع؟» — facts and drawings

## Facts
- Water boils at 100 °C at sea-level pressure; a pressure cooker at about 1 bar (15 psi) above atmospheric holds it near 120–121 °C.
- On Everest the air pressure is about a third of sea level and water boils near 70 °C.
- Cooking times drop to roughly a third (e.g. a beef stew: about 2 hours in a pot, about 30–40 minutes under pressure).
- Force on the lid: 1 bar × the area of a 22 cm lid (≈ 0.038 m²) ≈ 3,800 N ≈ 390 kg-force ("about 400 kilos").
- Safety: the weighted whistle sets the pressure; a second safety valve (plug) opens if the vent clogs (lentils, bean foam);
  many lids have a gasket-release slot that lets the gasket bulge out and vent; never open while pressurised.
- Denis Papin presented his "steam digester" to the Royal Society in 1679; early models exploded and he added the first
  steam safety valve (Wikipedia "Steam digester"; Chemistry World "Papin's digester").

## Drawings
The cooker is the common Egyptian stovetop kind: polished steel body, a long black handle on the body and one on the lid,
a weighted whistle on a vent pipe, a red safety plug. Drawn from general knowledge of the shape; no photo was reused.
