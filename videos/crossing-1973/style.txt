Speak in casual Egyptian Arabic (Cairo dialect), like a calm, heartfelt storyteller telling a true story to a friend late at night: the story of the October 1973 crossing of the Suez Canal and the engineer who broke the sand wall with water. Low, warm, intimate voice, close to the microphone, never excited or salesy, never like a YouTuber. Slow, unhurried pace with real pauses: a long pause at every "…", and a short breath between sentences. Let the tension build quietly through the early hours and the countdown to two o'clock, almost a whisper on "تخيّل إنك قاعد في القارب ده", then a deep, proud swell on "الله أكبر" and on "فتحته مية القناة". Gentle and warm on the small human details: the fasting soldiers, the sugarcane, breaking the fast on the other bank. End with quiet pride and hope on the last lines. Never use Modern Standard Arabic pronunciation; pronounce ق as a glottal stop like Cairenes.
