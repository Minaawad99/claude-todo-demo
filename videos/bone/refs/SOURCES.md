# Bone healing episode: visual references

- **Long bone, cut lengthwise:** "Illu long bone.jpg" (SEER Training, US National Cancer Institute, public domain, Wikimedia
  Commons). Gives the layers drawn in `boneBody()`: periosteum outside, compact cortex, yellow marrow in the medullary
  cavity, the nutrient artery entering through the cortex.
- **Compact and spongy bone:** "Illu compact spongy bone.jpg" (SEER Training, public domain, Wikimedia Commons): osteons
  run along the shaft (the dashed lines in the cortex), osteocyte lacunae (the small dark lenses), vessels through
  Volkmann canals (the branches into the cortex).
- **Fracture repair stages:** the four textbook stages as drawn in OpenStax *Anatomy and Physiology* 6.5 "Fractures: Bone
  Repair" (CC BY 4.0): fracture haematoma from torn vessels; internal callus in the marrow cavity and external callus round
  the outside, made of fibrocartilage from periosteal and endosteal cells; cartilage replaced by woven (trabecular) bone,
  working from the ends towards the gap; remodelling by osteoclasts and osteoblasts back to the original shape.
- **Hand and forearm X-ray:** the radius on the thumb side, the ulna on the little-finger side, two rows of carpals, five
  metacarpals, phalanges (2 in the thumb, 3 in each finger), following the «طب إزاي؟» X-ray video (sheet v07).
- **Arm cast:** a short arm cast from below the elbow to the palm crease, with the thumb out through a hole.

## Facts checked

- Haematoma forms in the first hours; soft (fibrocartilage) callus from about the end of the first week; hard callus from
  about week 2–3, an adult forearm is usually out of the cast at about 6–8 weeks; remodelling takes months to years.
- The progenitor cells for the external callus come from the periosteum.
- Children heal faster; smoking slows healing and raises the risk of non-union; calcium and vitamin D support it.
- "Healed bone is stronger than before" is a myth: the callus is thicker for a while, remodelling takes it back to the
  original shape and strength.
- Bone heals by regeneration with bone, not with a fibrous scar.
