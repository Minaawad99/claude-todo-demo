# Sources — اختراعات عبقرية… اختفت فجأة

1. Louis Le Prince — Leeds Museums: https://museumsandgalleries.leeds.gov.uk/blog-life-mystery-and-legacy-of-louis-le-prince-fylq ; Public Domain Review: https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/roundhay-garden-scene-1888 — Roundhay Garden Scene 1888; boarded Dijon→Paris express 16 Sep 1890, never seen again; declared dead 1897; Edison theory unproven.
2. Greek fire — Byzantine incendiary weapon (from c. 672), burned on water; recipe a state secret, lost. (Encyclopaedia Britannica "Greek fire").
3. Flexible glass (vitrum flexile) — Pliny, Natural History 36.66; Petronius, Satyricon 51: Tiberius had the maker executed. A legend: card says «الحكاية بتقول».
4. Damascus (wootz) steel — production faded by the 18th century and the method was lost; modern smiths approximate it. (Verhoeven et al.; Reibold et al., Nature 2006.) The silk-scarf story is a legend, so it's not in the card.
5. Silphium — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silphium ; Pliny NH 19.15: last stalk sent to Nero; worth its weight in silver; on Cyrene's coins. Extinction is debated.
6. Tesla's papers — History.com: https://www.history.com/news/nikola-tesla-files-declassified-fbi ; MuckRock: https://www.muckrock.com/news/archives/2018/mar/19/fbi-tesla-ii — died Jan 1943, Office of Alien Property seized his belongings within days; John G. Trump's report (30 Jan 1943) found nothing dangerous; FBI files released later.
7. Dymaxion car — Smithsonian: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/buckminster-fuller-was-good-ideas-terrible-car-design-180963983/ ; Darienite: https://darienite.com/?p=52784 — Oct 1933 crash at the Chicago fair; the other car belonged to a Chicago South Park commissioner, left before reporters arrived; investors pulled out, 3 built.
8. Tucker 48 — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_48 ; The Henry Ford: https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/sets/detail/preston-tucker-and-the-tucker-48 — 51 cars (50 + prototype), SEC/mail-fraud trial, acquitted Jan 1950, company already in receivership.
9. Stanley Meyer — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_fuel_cell ; Lead Stories: https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2021/06/fact-check-water-car-inventor-stanley-meyer-was-not-killed-by-pentagon-cause-of-death-a-brain-aneurysm.html — 1996 Ohio court: "gross and egregious fraud"; died 20 Mar 1998; brother's poisoning claim; coroner: ruptured cerebral aneurysm.
10. Rudolf Diesel — Wikipedia (SS Dresden): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Dresden_(1896) ; History.com: https://history.com/this-day-in-history/inventor-rudolf-diesel-vanishes — boarded at Antwerp 29 Sep 1913, cabin empty in the morning, body found about 10 days later, identified by his belongings, returned to the sea.

11. Starlite — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starlite ; BBC Science Focus: https://www.sciencefocus.com/science/whatever-happened-to-starlite — Maurice Ward, hairdresser, BBC 1990; died 2011, formula never published (family; Thermashield bought notes 2020).
12. Soybean car — The Henry Ford: https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/popular-research-topics/soybean-car — unveiled 13 Aug 1941; destroyed (per Lowell Overly, by E.T. Gregorie); WWII stopped the project; how much soy was in it is disputed.
13. Chrysler Turbine Car — Old Cars Weekly: https://www.oldcarsweekly.com/car-of-the-week-1963-chrysler-turbine-car ; Jalopnik: https://jalopnik.com/video-chrysler-turbine-cars-meeting-fate-in-crusher-5572703 — 55 built (1963), public test programme, most crushed, 9 survive.
14. GM EV1 — The Henry Ford: https://thehenryford.org/artifact/326534 — 1,117 built, leased only, recalled and crushed by 2003, ~40 to museums/universities.
15. Ulfberht swords — NOVA «Secrets of the Viking Sword» (2012): https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/video/secrets-of-the-viking-sword — crucible steel rare in Europe; origin debated (Persia/Central Asia vs. Europe).
16. Nathan Stubblefield — Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Stubblefield ; Murray State: https://www.murraystate.edu/academics/CollegesDepartments/CollegeOfScienceEngineeringandTechnology/CollegeOfSciencePrograms/instituteOfEngineering/ccit/nathanstubblefield/ — wireless telephone demo 1 Jan 1902 before ~1,000 people; later lived alone in a shack; died 1928.
17. Hindenburg — 6 May 1937 fire at Lakehurst, 36 dead; the ignition source is still debated (static discharge most accepted). (Wikipedia "Hindenburg disaster".)
18. Stradivari — died 1737; instruments sell for millions; no agreed explanation for their sound (wood treatment, varnish, design all proposed).
19. Lycurgus Cup — British Museum: 4th-century Roman, green in reflected light, red in transmitted light; gold–silver nanoparticles (Barber & Freestone, 1990).
20. Roman concrete — MIT, Science Advances, Jan 2023 (Seymour, Masic et al.): lime clasts and hot mixing give self-healing.
