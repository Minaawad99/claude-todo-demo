## Facts checked
- Electrochemical test strips: enzyme (glucose oxidase or glucose dehydrogenase) oxidises glucose; a mediator (e.g. ferricyanide /
  osmium complexes) carries the electrons to a printed carbon working electrode; the meter applies a small potential and measures the
  current, which is proportional to glucose (amperometry). Heller & Feldman, "Electrochemical Glucose Sensors and Their Applications in
  Diabetes Management", Chem. Rev. 2008.
- Strip build: PET base, screen-printed carbon tracks + contact pads, insulation, dry reagent, spacer forming a capillary channel, cover with vent.
- Accuracy: ISO 15197:2013 — 95 % of results within ±15 mg/dL below 100 mg/dL and within ±15 % at or above 100 mg/dL.
- Fruit/juice residue on the finger raises the reading: Hirose et al., Diabetes Care 2011 ("Glucose monitoring after fruit peeling").
- Heat, humidity and expired strips degrade the enzyme: manufacturers' strip storage instructions (FDA guidance on BGMS, 2020).
- Ames Reflectance Meter (Anton Clemens, 1970): read the colour of a Dextrostix strip by reflectance, about 1.2 kg, made for clinics.
