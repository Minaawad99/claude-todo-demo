It all starts at the pump.

A little fuel pump sends the gas from the tank up to the engine.

Inside the engine, there are these metal tubes called cylinders.

Most cars have four, six, or eight of them.

And inside each one, there's a piston, a metal plug that slides up and down.

Every piston runs the same four-step routine, over and over.

Step one: suck.

The piston slides down, a valve opens, and air rushes in.

Then an injector sprays in a fine mist of gas, like a perfume bottle.

Step two: squeeze.

The valve shuts, and the piston shoots up, crushing that mix 10× smaller.

Squeezed fuel burns way harder. That's the whole trick.

Step three: bang.

A spark plug zaps it with around 20,000 volts.

Boom. The mix explodes, hits around 4,000 degrees, and slams the piston down hard.

That's the only step that actually makes power.

Step four: blow.

The piston comes back up and pushes the burnt gas out the exhaust.

Suck, squeeze, bang, blow. Mechanics seriously call it that.

But wait, the piston only goes up and down. So how do the wheels spin?

Here's the cool part.

Every piston is hooked to a crankshaft, which works like the pedals on a bike.

Your legs push down, and the pedals go round. Same deal.

The pistons take turns firing, so the crankshaft never stops spinning.

And a camshaft up top opens every valve right on time.

From there, the spin goes through the transmission, which picks the right gear.

Then it runs through the axles, and boom, your wheels turn.

All those explosions make some crazy heat, though.

So coolant flows around the engine, and the radiator dumps that heat into the air.

And here's the wild part: less than a third of the energy in your gas actually moves the car.

The rest? It just turns into heat.
