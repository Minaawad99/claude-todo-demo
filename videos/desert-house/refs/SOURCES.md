# Sources — «تعمل بيت في الصحرا إزاي؟»

Style: «لو» (build-along), like «تعمل ملجأ نووي إزاي؟». Script and drawings are our own.

Facts checked:
- Metal (corrugated iron) roofs in strong sun reach surface temperatures around 65–80 °C (cool-roof studies, LBNL Heat Island Group).
- Western Desert of Egypt: summer highs regularly above 45 °C; large day–night swings.
- Shallow footings on loose sand settle; strip footings of stone/rubble taken down to firm ground (about 0.6–1 m) are the
  traditional and code practice for low mud-brick houses.
- Adobe / mud-brick (طين + رمل + تبن) walls 40–60 cm thick: time lag of heat through the wall roughly 8–12 hours, so the
  day's heat reaches the inside at night (thermal mass; Hassan Fathy, "Natural Energy and Vernacular Architecture", 1986).
- Mud-brick domes and Nubian vaults need no timber centring; Hassan Fathy used them at New Gourna (1945–48) because wood was scarce.
- Mud-brick vaults of the Ramesseum granaries in Luxor date from the reign of Ramesses II (~1250 BC), over 3,000 years old.
- Hot air rises and collects under a high dome, away from the occupants.
- Malqaf (wind catcher) faces the prevailing wind and drives air down into the room; Fathy placed wet charcoal / porous water
  jars (زير) in it to cool the air by evaporation by a few degrees.
- Courtyard houses with nearly blind outer walls and mashrabiya screens on inner openings (shade + airflow + sand protection).
- Western Desert groundwater: Nubian Sandstone Aquifer System; wells in the oases commonly go from about 100 m to over 1,000 m.
  Isotope dating gives ages from about 20,000 years to over a million years (recharged in wet "Green Sahara" periods).
- Measurements in traditional mud-brick houses in hot-arid Egypt show indoor peaks up to about 8–12 °C below outdoor peaks
  without mechanical cooling.
