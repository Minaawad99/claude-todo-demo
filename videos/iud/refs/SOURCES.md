# «اللولب بيمنع الحمل إزاي؟» — sources

## Facts in the script (standard medical references: WHO Family Planning handbook, ACOG and NHS IUD pages, FDA labels)
- Two kinds: the copper IUD (e.g. TCu380A / ParaGard) and the levonorgestrel IUD (e.g. Mirena), both small T frames about
  3 cm, fitted in the uterus by a clinician, with threads hanging through the cervix.
- Copper: releases copper ions that are toxic to sperm and reduce their motility and survival; the device sets off a sterile
  foreign-body reaction in the lining, with white blood cells that destroy sperm. It acts mainly before fertilisation.
- Levonorgestrel: thickens the cervical mucus so sperm cannot pass, and thins the lining (lighter periods).
- Effectiveness over 99 % for both. Copper: up to 10 years (TCu380A label); levonorgestrel: 3 to 8 years depending on the
  brand (Mirena now up to 8).
- After fitting: feel for the threads after each period; a check-up after the first period (about 3–6 weeks); see a doctor
  at once for severe pelvic pain, fever, unusual discharge or threads that can't be felt. No protection against STIs.
- Not an abortifacient: it prevents fertilisation; fertility returns quickly after removal.
- A copper IUD fitted within 5 days of unprotected sex is the most effective emergency contraception (over 99 %).
